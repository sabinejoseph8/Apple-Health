Clarivi Flow

The MVP's one core workflow: from a tester unplugging their phone in the morning to having acted on today's nudge and recorded whether they followed it.

Daily morning loop Tester view only Onboarding already done MVP scope only

Mapped from the MVP document. On days the nudge asked for a change, a push at 8pm asks whether the tester followed it, and the card shows the question from then; if still unanswered, the next morning's card asks (D32).

When the flow is complete

The tester has:

  • recorded how they feel today, before seeing the tool's verdict on the card;
  • seen today's status, the two or three metrics driving it, and one recommended action with its reason;
  • seen how today's score was worked out and how each metric is trending against its baseline;
  • acted on that recommendation during the day;
  • marked whether they followed it, on days the nudge asked for a change, from 8pm, either by answering the 8pm push or on the card. That answer is the evidence the Value target in D9 depends on.

Minimum screens and views

Six views cover the whole loop: five on the main path, and the weekly digest, which is optional.

Push notification

Main path · system surface

A lock-screen message with status and reason in one line; tapping it opens today's card in the home-screen app. On change days, a second push at 8pm asks whether they followed the nudge and opens the card on the question. On a morning with no sync by 11:30am, a reminder push asks the tester to run the Shortcut before noon; the nudge follows if it syncs. None of the follow-up or reminder wording carries health detail; all three need wording, not screens.

Daily check-in

Main path · first each day

One question with three answers: good, okay or off. Shown before today's status, with a way to skip it; the answer is stored with the day, can be changed until the next morning, and is never used in the score (D33).

Readiness card

Main path · the app's home

Status (ready, ease off, rest), the two or three drivers in plain words, the nudge and its reason, and when the data was synced. If today's check-in was skipped, it keeps a small prompt to answer it. On change days, from 8pm, it also carries Did you follow today's nudge? with yes and no above the morning's briefing; before 8pm it shows the nudge without the question; it asks about yesterday if that went unanswered, and shows sync or notification problems when there are any.

Why today

Main path · after the card

Titled with today's status: Why ease off today, Why rest today or Why you're ready today. One card per reading (heart rate variability, sleep, sleeping heart rate) with a line on what it measures, last night's value against your normal, a plain-words verdict and a 4-week chart with your normal range shaded. Tapping Show the numbers on a card gives its normal range, how far last night was from normal and how it compares with the last 4 weeks. A closing section, How today's status is decided, explains in plain words that each reading adds points when it is worse than normal, more for the readings that matter most; on tap it shows each reading's points, today's total and where ready, ease off and rest start. The weights themselves are not shown. Missing inputs are named.

Trend view

Main path · after Why today

One line per score metric with its baseline band and flagged days. Nights without data show as gaps.

Weekly digest

Optional · from the card

A template-written summary of the past week: what moved against baseline, what was flagged, how many days had data, and how many nudges were followed. A new one each week; the latest is reachable from the card.

In the MVP but outside this flow: account setup (you create each tester's account; no email is sent) and onboarding, sign-in (used at first setup and whenever a session has expired, since sessions are long-lived), and a small settings view (reissue the upload token, sign out everywhere, delete my data). The daily loop doesn't need any of them to run.

States that matter

Only the states the MVP rules produce. Descriptions say what the tester needs to see; the exact wording comes later.
