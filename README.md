Problem Statement
Apple Watch and the Health app collect an unusually rich stream of physiological and behavioral data – heart rate,
heart rate variability, sleep, workouts, weight, and gait – but present almost none of it as a trend. Each metric is
shown as an isolated daily card, with no way to see how it is drifting over time, how it relates to other metrics, or
whether today's reading is actually unusual for this person. This project builds a tool that ingests a user's exported
Apple Health data and turns it into a personalized, longitudinal picture of their own recovery, sleep, and training
patterns.

Who is your target audience?
Apple Watch users who train consistently (strength training, general fitness) and already track workouts and sleep,
but want to understand the patterns behind their numbers rather than just seeing isolated daily readings.

What problem are you solving?
Apple Health has no way to detect drift against a person's own baseline, surface relationships between metrics (e.g.
sleep and next-day recovery), or distinguish a normal day from an unusual one.

Why does this problem matter?
Without that visibility, early signs of under-recovery, overtraining, or illness go unnoticed until performance or health
has already suffered, and users can't tell whether a change they made (more sleep, a deload week) actually
worked.

What is the main thing the user should be able to do?
Load their Apple Health export and get back a personal dashboard that flags meaningful changes in their own data
and shows how their metrics move together over time.

What outcome do you want to help the user achieve?
Earlier, evidence-based awareness of their own recovery and readiness state, so training and lifestyle adjustments
are driven by their own data rather than generic guidance.
